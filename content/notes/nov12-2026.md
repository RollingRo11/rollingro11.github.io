### Other methods to detect memorization
Now that we don’t have a 1hr cap on what we can run, it would be nice to develop on some of the existing memorization literature to gain a better “mechanistic” understanding of memorization before developing a clear way to predict it. Below are a few ideas based on some reading I was doing today (**ordered most important to least important to read, for your convenience!**)
- I should note that this may also have us redefine what we consider robustness to be (besides just a drop in accuracy)

#### Understanding Memorization via Loss Curvature [(Merullo et al., 2025)](https://www.goodfire.com/research/understanding-memorization-via-loss-curvature#)

This is an obvious first choice, most of their interesting results are related to arithmetic! 

For a weight matrix $W$, they take the inputs to $W$, and the gradient wrt $W$’s **output**. They then calculate a covariance matrix for each of these terms (see figure for explanation).

![](/notes/nov12-2026/loss-curvature.png)

They then take the top eigenvectors (sorted by $G$’s top eigenvalues) of the $A$ matrix and project the acts of some datapoint $x$ onto them. If the projection is higher for the higher ranked eigenvectors, the datapoint is more general (AUROC not acc).

We tried this, and didn’t get very good results, but we I think there’s maybe more to develop on here (sorry, I won’t get enough time to write out my thoughts before sending this over... I still want to send it at a reasonable time.)

#### The Reasoning-Memorization Interplay in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction [(Hong et al., 2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.23084)

I really like this paper, and am maybe a bit sad I didn’t find it before the competition (I’m learning the importance of doing the reading a lot more 🫪)

They take a memorization-recall dataset and a reasoning-heavy dataset, and find vectors $\in \mathbb{R}^{\text{d}_{\text{model}}}$ that mediate between the two.

**They show that there is at least some difference between the two classes:**

![](/notes/nov12-2026/reasoning-memorization-split.png)


**And also show that steering towards “reasoning” improves performance on reasoning-heavy tasks!**

![](/notes/nov12-2026/reasoning-steering.png)

I think this was a cool result! More importantly, they show their steering results on GSM-Symbolic which is a perturbation dataset from Apple:

![](/notes/nov12-2026/gsm-symbolic-steering.png)

**(Higher $\alpha$ is steered further towards reasoning)**

Generally the reason I added these two specifically is because they both do a good job of presenting a new method to differentiate between memorized and generalized text, and I think we might want to do something similar for robust vs spurious examples.
