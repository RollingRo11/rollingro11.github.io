## Token Masking

For some model (we use Qwen3-4B), we want to learn a $T \times T$ matrix of causal importances $g \in [0,1]$. Some matrix entry $g_{p, s}$ is how much source token $s$ is *causally important* to destination token $p$ (triangular so $s < p$)

![](/notes/nov19-2026/masker.png)

### Causal importance model

The CI model is a 1-layer transformer.
- **Input:** The frozen model’s hidden states after every layer ($h^{(0)}$ (embeddings) to $h^{(36)}$). 
  - Each layer gets a linear map $W_k$ and the results are summed/scaled
  - $x_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_k W_k \bar{h}_i^{(k)}$.
- **Model** $N$ pre-norm transformer blocks with causal attention (default $N = 1$, width 256, 4 heads), then a final LayerNorm, which give $u$.
- **Scoring/gating** 
  - $g_{p,s} = \sigma (\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}} (Q u_p)^\top (K u_s) + c)$. 
  - One attention head's logits, but with a sigmoid per entry instead of a softmax over the row. 
  - $c$ is one learned scalar that starts at 2.5, so every importance starts near 0.92 (almost nothing masked).

### Masked Forward Pass

To explain destination $p$, take row $p$ as the mask $z$:
- $z_s = g_{p,s}$ for $s < p$, and $z_s = 1$ for $s \geq p$ (the predicting token is never masked).

Then rerun the same frozen model with that mask. In every transformer block $k$, attention reads token $s$'s keys and values (and its query) from $z_s h_s^{(k)} + (1 - z_s)\, b^{(k)}$, where $b^{(k)}$ is a learned per-layer baseline (the layer mean plus a learned offset in units of its std).
- The residual stream and the feed-forward are **not** changed, so the mask doesn't compound across the 36 layers.
- At $z_s = 0$, no other position can see anything of token $s$.

### Loss

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathrm{KL}\left( p(\cdot \mid x_{\leq p}) \,\|\, q(\cdot \mid x_{\leq p} \text{ masked by } z) \right) + c_{\text{sparsity}} \cdot \frac{1}{p} \sum_{s < p} g_{p,s}$$


### vs. DiffMask

- One model for all layers, instead of a probe per layer
- A mask per prediction (a matrix), instead of one per prompt
- No Hard Concrete sampling with a differential backward thingy
- A plain sparsity penalty instead of a Lagrangian constraint

## AIMO Baselines

The Gaussian probe clearly beats the CoT's token embeddings, answer agreement and CoT length. It ties most published probes and the bag of words. None of the probe's own ablations (other class models, max pooling) beats it.

### Setup

- **Data.** For each model: our labelled problems, plus the organizers' 33 (majority labels, 13 not robust). Qwen has 184 of ours (30 not robust), Skywork 177 (18), OLMo 95 (22) and DeepSeek 95 (18). Each problem uses its first 4 sampled CoTs, cut at 3,072 tokens. The label is per problem: robust or not robust.
- **What every probe sees.** The same 4 CoTs, labels and folds. Only the input or the classifier changes.
  - **Gaussian probe:** the hidden state averaged over tokens, then over the 4 CoTs; standardize + PCA-8, fitted without labels; then two Gaussians with a shared Ledoit-Wolf covariance. "Keep 25%" fits only on our problems nearest the organizers' cloud; "keep all" fits on every problem.
  - **Published probes:** 9 Gemini probes (Kramár et al.) and 4 FAR.AI probes, with their papers' hyperparameters (1,000 optimizer steps). They read each token through a per-layer PCA-512.
  - **Black-box:** bag of words (TF-IDF over the CoT text), answer agreement across the 4 CoTs, CoT length, and the CoT's token embeddings (layer 0).
- **Layer.** Fixed in advance at the halfway layer (Qwen 16, Skywork 14, OLMo 16, DeepSeek 18), except in the layer sweeps.
- **Three evaluation sets**, named in each title:
  - **Our problems (CV):** 5-fold cross-validation on our problems only[^4]
  - **Nearest 25% + organizers' (CV):** our problems and the organizers' 33 cross-validated as one dataset, scored on the organizers' 33 plus our 25% nearest their cloud.
  - **Organizers' 33:** fit once on all of ours, scored on the 33. This is not cross-validated.
- **Cross-validation.** Stratified group 5-fold with problems as groups (no problem in both train and test). Repeats: 3 for the fixed-layer bars, 2 for the layer sweeps and the Gaussian ablations, and 5 for the black-box figures. Scores are averaged over repeats.
- **Cutoff** (accuracy metrics only). Each fit holds out a grouped 20% of its training problems and picks the cutoff with the best balanced accuracy there. FAR.AI probes also pick their checkpoint on it. Test labels are never used.
- **Black-box figures** use nested CV on all data (ours + organizers'): an inner 4-fold picks the layer and cutoff.
- **Error bars.** 95% bootstrap over problems, 2,000 resamples.

### Gaussian probe:

We use the log-likelihood ratio to determine if a new problem is robust or not by looking at the model’s averaged hidden state of its CoT on that problem:

1. Average the layer-$L$ residual stream over each CoT's tokens, then over the problem's 4 rollout CoTs:
   $$x_i = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{c=1}^{4} \frac{1}{T_c}\sum_{t=1}^{T_c} h^{(L)}_{c,t}$$
2. Standardize $x_i$, then project onto the top 8 PCs, which gives $z_i \in \mathbb{R}^8$.
   1. (The PCA is fitted on our problems plus the organizers' problems)
3. Fit a Ledoit-Wolf Gaussian $(m, S)$ to the organizers' points. Keep our problems whose Mahalanobis distance $\sqrt{(z_i - m)^\top S^{-1} (z_i - m)}$ is in the nearest 25% to the organizers’.
4. On the kept problems, take the means per-class $\mu_N$ (not robust) and $\mu_R$ (robust), and one shared covariance $\Sigma$ pooled from the within-class residuals.
5. **Score = log-likelihood ratio.**
   $$s(z) = \log\frac{\mathcal{N}(z;\mu_N,\Sigma)}{\mathcal{N}(z;\mu_R,\Sigma)} = \tfrac{1}{2}\left[d_R^2(z) - d_N^2(z)\right] = w^\top z + b, \qquad w = \Sigma^{-1}(\mu_N - \mu_R)$$
   1. (Here $d_k^2(z) = (z - \mu_k)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (z - \mu_k)$ is the squared Mahalanobis distance to class $k$) (this is LDA).
6. "Not robust" if $s(z) >$ some handpicked $\tau$. In our evaluations, $\tau$ maximizes balanced accuracy on a held-out 20% of the training problems.
