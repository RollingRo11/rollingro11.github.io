## Model Organisms of Varied Latent Reasoning

***Recall our reasoning quadrant from before:***
![](/notes/nov19-2026/reasoning-quadrants.png)

Something me and Gabriele spoke about during [Friday’s meeting](https://docs.google.com/document/d/19BwCsrprdTGSbjLhsG2pRE143pqM2TjG5JSbWrHom7M/edit?usp=sharing) is that while it’s cool to be able to show the transition between activeness and spuriousness/reactiveness, we lack a clean way to show the transition between explicit and implicit reasoning. 

**So Gabriele proposed the following idea.** Take a model’s CoT on math problems:
- Have a judge rank the chunks (sentences, or some other delineation of the CoT) by how important they are
- Edit the CoT, such that the least important $k$ steps are removed, but their results stay in the CoT.
  - Do this at a few levels of detail
- Train the model on the edited CoTs via distillation, so the pruned steps have to happen inside the weights rather than through the CoT
- End up with 3-5 levels of model, where each model lies along the “explicit-implicit” axis from above.
- To ensure stability, rather than asking the CoT to be shorter we can have the model use dots or some other standard token just to have space to reason latently.


### Fuller proposal, with methodology

We want to take an existing model, and train variants such that each variant has an increasing level of “CoT obfuscation[^1]”

#### Methodology

The setup here is invariably much cleaner. The model, without any pressure, seems to already reason less in the CoT on higher loop counts! We could just apply harsher pressure on the length of the CoT while attempting to keep performance on some data, say, math.

### The tradeoffs between using normal and looped transformers

The difference here is the **kind** of test-time reasoning we’re making model organisms of:
- The dots idea models steganographic reasoning, where the model has a “performative” CoT that uses tokens as a means to have more space to move information around in
- The inkling idea models “latent” reasoning, where more literally happens in the weights (and no-CoT performance rises)

While these aren’t mutually exclusive in practice, they probably should be for our case (unless y’all think it would be a good idea to)

## Using attention to predict spuriousness

**Hypothesis**
- *Tokens that are spurious are built by more local context, rather than more global context*
- Concretely: I assume that tokens in reasoning that are spurious, or not influenced by the rest of the reasoning chain, will attribute or attend more to nearby tokens than far away ones.

### Methodology

There are two ways to measure attention based “distance”

#### Mean Attention Distance
For a destination token $i$, and source tokens $j \leq i$, the attention distance (for attention head $h$ at layer $l$) is:
$$
\alpha^{(l,h)}_{i,j}\,(i - j)
$$

Where $\alpha$ is the attention weight (post-softmax) between the query of token $i$ ($q_i$) and the key of token $j$ ($k_j$), for head $h$ at layer $l$

**To get the overall average per-token:**

$$
\text{avg\_distance}(i) = \frac{1}{LH} \sum_{l} \sum_{h} \frac{1}{i} \sum_{j \le i} \alpha^{(l,h)}_{i,j}\,(i - j)
$$

#### averaged ALTI/IFR composed attention information flow

Recall that in IFR, we calculate every additive operation in the residual stream as a sum of per-input terms. These per input terms, at some token representation $x^{lA}_i$ (the token representation after layer $l$’s attention block) is made up of per input terms:
- The residual stream representation of the token from the previous layer $x^{l-1}_i$
- A term per-head, per token $\alpha^{(l,h)}_{i,j} f^{(l,h)}$

Comparing each of these to the final $x^{lA}_i$ gives us an importance score for each per-input term. So we reuse our above average distance calculation, replacing attention weights for importance scores between tokens:

$$
\text{avg\_distance}(i) = \frac{1}{LH} \sum_{l} \sum_{h} \frac{1}{i} \sum_{j \le i} \text{IFR-importance}^{(l,h)}(i,j) (i - j)
$$
